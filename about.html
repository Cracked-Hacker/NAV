About NAV

Coaching, science, and better training tools.

I started NAV to bring my work in coaching, exercise science, and software together. The goal is simple: help people train with a clear plan, understand what they're doing, and keep useful records of their progress. Courses and certifications are still in development.

Founder · Coach Nate

Meet Coach Nate

Nate graduated from Western Washington University in 2023 with a B.S. in Kinesiology (pre-healthcare specialization), a B.A. in Biology, and a minor in Chemistry. His background includes fitness coaching, clinical support work, laboratory research, anatomy teaching, and a coauthored peer-reviewed publication.

Nate is a NASM Certified Personal Trainer (CPT). His certificate lists May 29, 2025 as the award date and May 29, 2027 as the expiration date.

Why NAV exists

Better tools should support better judgment.

Coaching often spreads programming, workouts, communication, and progress tracking across disconnected tools. NAV aims to make those tasks easier without replacing the coach or turning estimates into facts. The general-science educational work is being developed and reviewed separately from the software.

NAV provides fitness coaching and education—not medical diagnosis, physical therapy, or treatment.

Experience behind NAV

Coaching, research, and hands-on experience.

Experience and approximate hours are drawn from Nate’s 2026 résumé and CV.

Coaching and athletics

Personal trainer, strength coach, and group fitness instructor at Blinnergy in Woodinville since June 2025; fitness trainer with EXOS at Google's Seattle South Lake Union campus since March 2026. Across both positions, Nate has more than 2,000 coaching hours on his résumé.

Nate has also coached youth lacrosse and powerlifting, volunteered in athletics, and competed in powerlifting.

Patient care and rehabilitation settings

Worked as a certified nursing assistant at North Cascades Health and Rehabilitation and through Staffers/Avalon Healthcare, totaling more than 1,600 hours of care work. He also worked more than 800 hours as a clinical aide at FYZICAL Therapy & Balance Centers.

Scientific research and publication

Spent more than 300 hours in Dr. Catherine Clark's environmental chemistry laboratory at WWU, using microscopy, DAPI staining, spectroscopy, and quantitative image analysis. Presented work on ethanol and acetaldehyde cycling in estuarine waters at an American Chemical Society conference in March 2023, as documented in his résumé and original poster.

Later contributed literature review and manuscript work as a University of Washington School of Social Work research assistant with Dr. Angelique Day. He is a named coauthor of a 2024 journal article on Medicaid contraceptive-care claims among youth in foster care. View the publication by DOI →

Teaching and community science

Undergraduate teaching assistant for WWU's BIOL 348 Anatomy & Physiology lab in 2022, with more than 60 hours of teaching experience. He also worked on community biotechnology curriculum work with Sound Bio Labs and observational shadowing in the University of Washington Yang Lab.
