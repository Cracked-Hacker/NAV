Science & evidence

Use evidence. Keep the uncertainty.

NAV uses research to improve questions and decisions—not to manufacture certainty. Evidence matters alongside the athlete’s goals, history, preferences, constraints, and response to training.

Evidence standard

Stronger evidence gets more weight.

No single study automatically becomes a coaching rule. NAV looks for the best available body of evidence and then asks how well it applies to the actual decision.

1

Systematic reviews & meta-analyses

Useful for understanding a body of research when the included studies and methods are appropriate.

2

Consensus statements & guidelines

Useful when credible expert groups transparently synthesize evidence and practical considerations.

3

Controlled trials

Useful for testing causal questions, while still requiring attention to population, intervention, duration, and outcome.

4

Observational evidence & inference

Useful context when stronger evidence is unavailable, but conclusions should stay proportionate to the evidence.

From paper to practice

A finding is not the same thing as a prescription.

NAV separates what research reports from what a coach chooses to do with it.

Ask

What did the evidence actually measure?

Population, comparison, duration, outcome, effect size, uncertainty, and study quality all matter.

Translate

Does it fit this person and this goal?

A useful average effect may still need adjustment for equipment, schedule, training age, preference, or tolerance.

Observe

What happens after the choice?

Training response, adherence, performance, and feedback provide new context for the next decision.

What NAV does not claim

Evidence-informed coaching is not medical care and it is not a guarantee.

NAV does not diagnose or treat medical conditions, guarantee strength or body-composition outcomes, or present estimates as direct measurements. When a question belongs with a licensed healthcare professional, NAV’s coaching and educational scope does not replace that care.
