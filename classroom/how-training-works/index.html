How Training Actually Works

Why does your body adapt? Specificity, progressive overload, fatigue, recovery, and adaptation form the basic logic of training.

Key takeawayYour body adapts specifically to the demands you repeatedly place on it.

Lesson 1

Training is specific

Adaptations are strongly influenced by what you practice: the movements, loads, velocities, ranges of motion, and energy-system demands you repeatedly train. Getting better at one task does not guarantee the same improvement in every other task.

Lesson 2

Progressive overload is a process

As your capacity improves, training must remain sufficiently challenging to keep producing the intended stimulus. Progression can involve load, repetitions, sets, range of motion, technique, density, or a more demanding exercise—not simply adding weight every workout.

Lesson 3

Fatigue is not the same as adaptation

A hard session can temporarily reduce performance. The goal is not to create the most fatigue possible; it is to apply enough useful training stress, recover, and repeat the process consistently enough to improve.

Quick check

Can you apply the idea?

Which statement best describes progressive overload?
Reveal answer

Best answer: Gradually keep training sufficiently challenging as your capacity improves.

Progressive overload is broader than load alone. The training demand needs to remain appropriate as you adapt, and that can be progressed in several ways.

Solo gym lab

Find the stimulus in your workout

Use a workout you are doing today or one you recently completed.

  1. Choose one main exercise from the workout.
  2. State the main quality you are trying to improve: strength, hypertrophy, power, skill, endurance, or another clear goal.
  3. Record the load, reps, sets, and effort you used.
  4. Name one variable you could progress later if the current prescription becomes too easy.
