Getting Strong

Why does strength increase? Practice, coordination, neural adaptations, muscle size, and loading all contribute to strength development.

Key takeawayStrength is specific and reflects skill, neural adaptations, and muscular adaptations.

Lesson 1

Strength is a skill as well as a physical capacity

Improving a lift involves becoming better at coordinating and producing force in that specific task. This is one reason practicing the movement you want to improve matters.

Lesson 2

Heavier loading is especially useful for maximal strength

Many loading ranges can improve strength, but higher-load resistance training is generally more effective when the goal is maximizing one-repetition-maximum strength.

Lesson 3

More muscle can increase strength potential

Muscle size is not the only determinant of strength, but increasing contractile tissue can contribute to a greater capacity to produce force. Long-term strength programs often combine specific heavy practice with enough hypertrophy-oriented work to support that capacity.

Quick check

Can you apply the idea?

If your main goal is to improve a one-repetition maximum, which approach is generally most specific?
Reveal answer

Best answer: Regular practice with relatively heavy loads while managing fatigue.

Maximal strength is highly specific. Relatively heavy loading and practice of the relevant movement are generally useful when 1RM strength is the target.

Solo gym lab

Perform a technically strong submaximal set

Use an exercise you already know how to perform safely. Do not test a true maximum for this lab.

  1. Choose a load you expect to leave about 2–4 good repetitions in reserve.
  2. Perform one controlled working set with consistent technique.
  3. Record the load, repetitions, and estimated RIR or RPE.
  4. Write down one technical feature you would want to reproduce next time.
