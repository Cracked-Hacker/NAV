Choosing Good Exercises

What makes an exercise worth doing? Specificity, target musculature, range of motion, stability, comfort, progression, skill, and equipment all affect exercise fit.

Key takeawayExercise selection should serve the goal and the individual rather than a universal ranking.

Lesson 1

Start with the goal

An exercise is only “good” relative to what you want it to accomplish. A movement chosen for maximal strength practice may be different from one chosen to isolate a muscle, develop power, or reduce unnecessary skill demands.

Lesson 2

Favor repeatable exercises

Exercises are easier to evaluate when setup, technique, range of motion, and loading can be reproduced consistently. Repeatability makes progression and performance trends more meaningful.

Lesson 3

Individual fit matters

Anthropometry, training history, symptoms, equipment, preference, and skill can change how useful an exercise is for a particular person. Discomfort that is sharp, worsening, or concerning is not something a learner should simply push through for this course.

Quick check

Can you apply the idea?

Which is the strongest first question when choosing an exercise?
Reveal answer

Best answer: What specific goal is this exercise supposed to serve?

Exercise selection should begin with the intended outcome, then consider whether the movement is a practical fit for the person and setting.

Solo gym lab

Run the NAV exercise screen

Choose three exercises that could plausibly train the same target muscle or movement goal.

  1. Score each exercise informally for goal specificity, stability, usable range of motion, comfort, and ease of progression.
  2. Perform a safe trial set of each if the exercise is familiar to you.
  3. Identify the main tradeoff of each option.
  4. Choose the best fit for your current goal—not the exercise with the most points in every category.
