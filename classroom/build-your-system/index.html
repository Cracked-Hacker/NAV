Starter Kit · Module 8

Build Your Training System

How do I use all of this? Define the goal, choose a program, train consistently, record outcomes, recover, evaluate, and adjust when the evidence supports a change.

Key takeawayGood training is a repeatable decision process you can observe, evaluate, and improve.

Lesson 1

Start with an outcome you can evaluate

A useful goal should tell you what success looks like. “Get better” is vague; improving a lift, adding muscle, increasing work capacity, or completing a specific training target gives you something to monitor.

Lesson 2

Do not change the plan before you have information

Frequent random changes make it harder to know what is working. Keep enough consistency to observe trends, while still changing the program when performance, recovery, goals, or constraints meaningfully change.

Lesson 3

Track what matters to the goal

Useful records can include load, repetitions, RIR/RPE, volume, body mass, measurements, symptoms, sleep, or adherence. The best metric is the one that helps answer a real coaching question rather than creating more data for its own sake.

Quick check

Can you apply the idea?

What is the best reason to track training data?
Reveal answer

Best answer: To answer whether the plan is producing the intended result and guide future decisions.

Tracking is useful when it helps evaluate the plan and informs decisions. More data is not automatically better data.

Solo gym lab

Build your first NAV training loop

Create one simple system you can actually repeat.

  1. Write one primary training goal for the next block.
  2. Choose one workout or training template that serves that goal.
  3. Choose 2–4 metrics you will record consistently.
  4. Write one rule for when you would progress, maintain, or modify the plan.
