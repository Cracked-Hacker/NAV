Sets, Reps, RIR & RPE

How hard should you train? RIR and RPE help describe effort and can make training prescriptions more adaptable to day-to-day performance.

Key takeawayEffort matters, but every set does not need to reach momentary muscular failure.

Lesson 1

RIR describes how many good reps you think remain

An estimate of 2 RIR means you believe you could complete about two additional repetitions with acceptable technique before reaching momentary failure. It is an estimate, so calibration improves with practice.

Lesson 2

RPE describes overall set difficulty

In resistance training, a common RPE scale links RPE 10 to no repetitions remaining, RPE 9 to roughly one repetition remaining, and so on. RPE and RIR are related tools rather than separate physiological mechanisms.

Lesson 3

Failure is a tool, not a requirement

Current evidence does not show that every set must reach momentary muscular failure to produce hypertrophy. Stopping somewhat short of failure can still be effective and may help manage fatigue, especially on demanding exercises.

Quick check

Can you apply the idea?

What does 2 RIR mean?
Reveal answer

Best answer: You think you could perform about two more good repetitions before failure.

RIR is an estimate of repetitions remaining before momentary failure with acceptable technique.

Solo gym lab

Calibrate your RIR

Use a safe, familiar exercise where stopping a set is easy. Avoid high-risk maximal attempts.

  1. Perform one set you estimate to be about 3 RIR.
  2. Rest normally, then perform a similar set you estimate to be about 2 RIR.
  3. If appropriate for the exercise and your experience, perform a third set near 1 RIR.
  4. Record how bar speed, breathing, technique, and perceived difficulty changed across the sets.
