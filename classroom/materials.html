How publishing works

Simple for students. Controlled for instructors.

The public Classroom is a read-and-learn surface. Publishing controls stay in authenticated NAV Coach so students never receive public write access.

1 · Instructor

Add the material once.

Upload a document, presentation, image, spreadsheet, or supported video file—or use a Drive, YouTube, Vimeo, or other approved HTTPS link.

2 · Organize

Attach it to the right place.

Choose the course, week or module, content type, instructions, and due date when one is actually needed.

3 · Student

See only what is published.

Students get the clean course-facing result without access to drafts, upload controls, or private instructor storage.

Evidence classes

Say how sure we are.

NAV’s curriculum explicitly separates strong outcome evidence from emerging evidence, mechanistic explanation, and unsupported claims.

A

High confidence

Convergent high-quality reviews/meta-analyses and/or directly relevant controlled outcome evidence.

B

Moderate confidence

Multiple relevant human studies with meaningful but important limitations.

C

Emerging

Plausible, early, or mixed human evidence; not strong enough for universal prescription.

D

Mechanistic

Useful for explanation and hypothesis generation, but insufficient alone to prove superior long-term programming.

X

Unsupported / refuted

A claim that should not be presented as established.

Three depths

Quick Answer → Learn → Deep Dive

Concepts should support a 30–90 second answer, a focused 5–12 minute lesson, and optional deeper mechanisms, methods, controversies, and primary literature.

Canonical concept

One knowledge object, many uses.

FAQs, lessons, quizzes, labs, assignments, files, and videos should point back to the same concept instead of duplicating contradictory explanations.
